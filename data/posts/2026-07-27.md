## VARIANT A — Financial Hook

A single Club Pilates operator just sold 34 units to private equity.

Eagle Merchant Partners bought a majority stake in that operator. Then bolted on a 6-unit and a 3-unit franchisee alongside it.

That is how buyers build in fitness right now.

Here is what changed. Buyers got pickier. The gap between an A operator and a C operator is wider than it was two years ago.

But strong A and B businesses? Still trading at the same solid multiples.

What matters most now is stability, room to grow, and a management team that runs the business without you.

As someone who works with franchise buyers and sellers every day, I can tell you the owner-dependent business is the one that gets marked down.

If the operation only works because you are there, buyers see risk. They pay less.

The owners getting top dollar built a team and systems that keep humming when they step away.

Three things to watch:

- Whether your business runs without you in the building
- Room to add units or grow in your territory
- Consistent revenue month after month, not one-time spikes

## VARIANT B — Contrarian Hook

Two identical fitness studios. Same revenue. One sells for way more.

Why?

Buyers stopped paying for revenue alone. They pay for what happens after they take over.

A private equity firm just backed a 34-unit Club Pilates operator. They also grabbed a 6-unit and a 3-unit deal at the same time.

Here is what they cared about most: room to grow, a team that can run without the owner, and a business that stays steady through any economy.

For a small owner, this matters more than you think.

The gap between an "A" business and a "B" business is getting wider. Same size. Very different price.

As someone who works with franchise buyers and sellers every day, I can tell you the owners who cash out big are not the busiest ones.

They are the ones who built something a buyer can run without them.

If your business needs you in the building every day to survive, that scares buyers. And scared buyers pay less.

Three things to watch:

- Can your business run without you?
- Is there room to add units nearby?
- Does your revenue hold steady in a slow month?

# POST 2 — Personal Post

**Topic:** Why waiting until you're burned out is the worst time to sell

---

The worst time to sell is when you can't stand walking into your own business.

I've closed 100+ franchise deals over 20 years. And I can tell you the sellers who wait too long almost always leave money on the table.

Here's why.

Burnout shows up in your numbers. You stop pushing sales. You defer maintenance. You let good staff walk. Buyers see all of it.

By the time you're ready to be done, your business often reflects how you feel. Tired.

I worked with an owner last year who waited two years too long. Great location. Loyal customers. But the last 18 months of declining revenue scared off buyers and dropped his valuation more than he ever expected.

If he'd sold while he still cared, he would have walked away with a lot more.

The lesson is simple.

The best time to sell is when the business is strong and you still have energy left. Not when you're running on empty.

Plan the exit before you need it.
