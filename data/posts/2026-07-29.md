## VARIANT A — Financial Hook

A 34-unit Club Pilates operator just got bought by private equity.

They bought a 6-unit and a 3-unit operator too.

Here's what stood out. The buyer said A-grade and B-grade businesses are trading at nearly the same price right now.

But C-grade businesses? Falling behind.

So what makes you an A or a B? Three things. A stable, proven business. Room to grow. And a management team that runs without you.

That last one matters most for small owners.

As someone who works with franchise buyers and sellers every day, I can tell you the "management team" point trips up more owners than anything else.

If the business only works because you show up every day, buyers see risk. Risk means a lower price.

The owners who get top dollar built a business that runs without them. That is the whole game.

Three things to watch:

- Can your business run for two weeks without you? If not, start fixing that now.
- Do you have clear room to grow, either new locations or acquisitions?
- Are your numbers clean and easy for a buyer to trust?

## VARIANT B — Contrarian Hook

Two fitness businesses with the same revenue can sell for very different prices.

Investors just poured money into a 34-unit Club Pilates operator.

But here's what they said out loud: the gap between an "A" business and a "C" business is getting wider.

Same units. Same brand. Very different valuations.

Why? Buyers are digging into the boring stuff. Your management team. Your room to grow. How clean your books are.

For a 1-20 unit owner, this matters more than you think.

You might assume your business is worth what the guy down the road got. It's not. Buyers are pickier now.

As someone who works with franchise buyers and sellers every day, I can tell you the "A" sellers all have one thing in common.

They can step away and the business keeps running.

If the whole operation lives in your head, you're a "C" asset. That costs you real money at the table.

Three things to watch:

- Can your business run without you for 30 days?
- Do you have open territory left to grow into?
- Are your books clean enough for a buyer to trust?

# POST 2 — Personal Post

**Topic:** Why the best time to think about your exit is right now

---

The worst time to sell is when you have to.

I have watched this play out for 20 years.

An owner gets tired, or sick, or just burned out. Then they call me wanting to sell fast. And a rushed sale almost always leaves money on the table.

The best deals I close start years before the owner is ready to leave.

Why? Because we have time to clean up the books. Time to fix the manager gap so the business runs without you. Time to sell when the numbers are strong, not when you are running on empty.

I had a seller last year who called me two years before he wanted out. We spent that time getting his unit ready. When we finally listed it, he sold for well above what he expected.

Compare that to owners who wait until they are done. They take what they can get.

Here is the takeaway. Thinking about your exit early is not being negative. It is being smart. You build more value and you get more choices.
