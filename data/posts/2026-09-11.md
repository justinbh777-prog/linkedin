They bought it because the operator knew the model cold and had room to grow.

Here's what most owners miss.

Buyers don't just pay for the units you have today. They pay for the runway you leave behind.

Open territory. A proven playbook. An owner who built something repeatable.

As someone who works with franchise buyers and sellers every day, I'll tell you the biggest value driver isn't your revenue.

It's whether the business runs without you.

Three things to watch:

# POST 2 — Personal Post

**Topic:** Why franchise owners underestimate how long a sale takes

---

"I want to sell by summer."

I hear this in January all the time. Then summer comes and goes.

Here's the truth nobody tells franchise owners: a good sale takes 6 to 12 months. Sometimes longer.

Most people think it's like selling a house. List it, get an offer, close in 60 days. It doesn't work that way.

Buyers do deep homework. They dig into your books, your leases, your franchise agreement. The franchisor has to approve the new owner. That alone can add months.

Last year I worked with an owner who wanted a quick exit. His financials were messy. We spent three months just cleaning them up before we could talk to a single buyer.

That delay didn't cost him time. It cost him leverage.

Here's what I tell every owner: start early. The best exits happen when you plan a year or two out, not when you're burned out and ready to be done.

Time is the thing that gives you options.
