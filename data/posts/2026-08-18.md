That's Voda Cleaning & Restoration's growth from early 2024 to the end of 2025.

Fast growth looks great on paper. But it changes what the numbers mean.

When a brand adds 100+ locations in 24 months, most of those units are new. New units drag down the average revenue you see in the FDD.

So a young location can make a growing brand look weaker than it is.

Here's what this means if you own a service or cleaning franchise.

Don't judge your value by brand-wide averages. Buyers care about YOUR numbers, not the system's.

A mature, well-run unit inside a fast-growing brand often sells for a premium. Why? Because you've proven the model works while newer owners are still ramping.

Your track record is the asset. The brand's momentum is just the tailwind.

Three things to watch:

## VARIANT B — Contrarian Hook

Fast growth is not the same as easy money. Keep that in mind.

Voda Cleaning & Restoration went from 2 units in early 2024 to 105 by the end of 2025.

That is explosive growth for a young brand in the cleaning and restoration space.

But new units do not have a track record yet. And a franchise system that young has almost no resale history.

For the 1-20 unit owner, this matters. When you go to sell, buyers look at how similar units have performed and sold before. A short history means fewer comps and more uncertainty on your number.

As someone who works with franchise buyers and sellers every day, I can tell you buyers pay for proof, not promise.

A brand growing this fast is exciting. But the owners who win are the ones who build steady, provable earnings while everyone else chases the next unit.

Slow and boring sells. Every time.

Three things to watch:

- How many of those 105 units are still open in two years
- Whether early owners hit the revenue numbers in the FDD
- If any resales start showing up in the system

# POST 2 — Personal Post

**Topic:** Why owner-dependent businesses always sell for less

---

Buyers don't want to buy your job.

That's the hard truth about owner-dependent businesses. And it shows up directly in the sale price.

Over 20 years, I've watched this pattern hold every single time. The more the business needs you, the less it's worth to someone else.

Because a buyer isn't just buying revenue. They're buying what happens after you leave.

If you're the one holding the key vendor relationships, training every new hire, and solving every problem, then you're not selling a business. You're selling chaos with your name on it.

I once had two owners with nearly identical numbers. Same revenue. Same location count. One had a strong general manager and clean systems. The other ran everything himself.

The first one sold for almost 40% more.

Same business on paper. Very different value in real life.

So here's the takeaway. Start removing yourself now. Build the team. Write down the systems. Make the business run without you.

That work pays you back at the closing table.
