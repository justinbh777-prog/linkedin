# POST 2 — Personal Post

**Topic:** The most common thing that kills a franchise deal at the last minute

---

Most deals don't die over price.

They die over messy books.

I've watched owners agree on a number, shake hands, and feel great. Then the buyer's team starts digging into the financials.

That's where it falls apart.

Cash transactions that never made it onto the books. Personal expenses run through the business. Owner add-backs that can't be proven with a single document.

The seller knows the business made money. But if you can't show it clearly, the buyer won't pay for it.

Had a deal last year where the owner swore the store cleared more than the tax returns showed. He was probably right. But we couldn't prove it, so the buyer discounted the offer and the seller walked away frustrated.

That gap cost him real money.

Here's the takeaway. Clean up your books two years before you sell, not two weeks before. Stop mixing personal and business spending. Document your add-backs as you go.

Buyers pay for what they can verify. Not for what you tell them.
