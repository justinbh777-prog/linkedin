## VARIANT A — Financial Hook

32 gyms. The rights to open 70 more. Bought by a private equity firm this summer.

That's the story of Bravo Fit, an Australian Planet Fitness operator.

Here's what jumps out. This team started with 34 gyms in Maryland and grew nationwide over a decade.

Then they took those learnings overseas. Now a bigger buyer scooped them up.

Why does this matter to you?

Because the buyer already owned another Planet Fitness operator with 200+ U.S. locations. They bought what they already understood.

As someone who works with franchise buyers and sellers every day, I see this pattern constantly.

Buyers pay more for operators inside brands they already know. Familiarity lowers their risk. Lower risk means a higher price for you.

The best exits aren't random. They happen when the right buyer already wants what you have.

Three things to watch:

- Buyers who already own units in your brand are your most likely purchasers.
- Development rights (unopened territory) add real value to your sale.
- Proven operators get premium offers, even at small unit counts.

# POST 2 — Personal Post

**Topic:** Why waiting until you're burned out is the worst time to sell

---

The worst time to sell is when you finally want to.

I've closed over 100 franchise deals in 20+ years. And I can tell you the pattern almost never changes.

Owners wait until they're exhausted. Until they dread going in. Until the business feels like a weight instead of a win.

By then, it usually shows in the numbers.

When you're burned out, you stop reinvesting. You let the small things slide. Revenue flattens. Staff turnover creeps up.

Buyers notice all of it. And they pay less because of it.

I worked with an owner last year who ran three units. Great locations. But he'd checked out mentally two years earlier. The decline was baked in by the time he called me.

He still sold. Just for a lot less than he could have.

Here's the takeaway.

The best time to sell is when things are still good. When you have energy left and the numbers are strong. That's when you have leverage.

Selling from strength beats selling from exhaustion every time.
