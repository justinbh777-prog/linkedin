# POST 2 — Personal Post

**Topic:** What buyers pay a premium for that has nothing to do with revenue

---

Two businesses. Same revenue. One sold for 40% more.

The difference had nothing to do with the numbers on the P&L.

It was how the owner showed up in the business.

Buyers pay a premium when they can see themselves stepping out of the day-to-day. If the owner is the one answering every call, closing every sale, and holding every relationship, that is a red flag. It means the business is really just a job.

The best price I ever got for a seller came from a franchise where the owner worked maybe ten hours a week. He had a strong manager. Clear systems. A team that ran without him.

That business wasn't worth more because it made more. It was worth more because the buyer was buying freedom, not a second job.

Here is the takeaway. Start building yourself out of the operation long before you plan to sell. Train your people. Write down your processes. Make yourself replaceable.

That is what buyers actually pay for.
