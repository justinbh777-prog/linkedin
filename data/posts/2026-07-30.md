## VARIANT A — Financial Hook

A 34-unit Club Pilates operator just got bought by private equity.

They didn't buy it alone. They paired it with a 6-unit and a 3-unit operator in the same deal.

That's how buyers think now. They want a base to build on.

Fitness valuations have cooled from the post-pandemic highs. But good businesses are still selling well.

Here's the shift. Buyers are pickier. The gap between an A business and a C business is wider than ever.

What separates them? Stability. Room to grow. And a management team that runs the place without the owner.

As someone who works with franchise buyers and sellers every day, that last one matters most for you.

If your business only works because you show up, that's a C.

Build a team that runs it without you, and you jump to an A. Same revenue. Higher price.

Three things to watch:

- Can your business run for 30 days without you? Buyers check.
- Do you have open territory or room to add units? That's white space, and it pays.
- Is your revenue recurring? Memberships beat one-time sales at the closing table.

# POST 2 — Personal Post

**Topic:** The most common thing that kills a franchise deal at the last minute

---

Most franchise deals don't die over price.

They die over the lease.

I've watched fully agreed deals fall apart in the final two weeks because nobody looped in the landlord early enough.

Here's what happens. The buyer and seller shake hands. The numbers work. Everyone's excited.

Then the landlord needs to approve the lease transfer. And suddenly there's a personal guarantee the buyer won't sign. Or the landlord wants to renegotiate terms. Or they just take six weeks to respond.

Deals lose momentum fast. When a closing drags, buyers get cold feet and start finding other problems.

I had a seller last year with a clean business and a motivated buyer. The lease had 14 months left and the landlord dragged his feet on renewal. That deal almost collapsed twice before we got it done.

Here's the takeaway. If you're thinking about selling, pull out your lease today. Check the term, the transfer clause, and the guarantee language.

Fix the messy stuff before a buyer ever sees it.

The best time to solve a lease problem is before you have a buyer waiting on it.
