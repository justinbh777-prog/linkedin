# POST 2 — Personal Post

**Topic:** What SBA lenders look for that most sellers never think about

---

Most deals don't die over price. They die at the bank.

Here's what sellers miss: the SBA lender isn't just looking at your numbers. They're looking at whether a stranger can run your business without you.

That's the part nobody prepares for.

I've watched profitable stores struggle to get financed because the owner was the business. All the key relationships, the vendor deals, the staff loyalty - it all lived in one person's head.

Lenders see that as risk. Risk makes them nervous. Nervous lenders slow deals down or walk away.

Had a seller last year with great cash flow. Looked like an easy close. But he handled every big account himself and never documented a thing. The bank wanted proof the business could survive his exit. We spent three extra months building systems that should have existed years earlier.

The takeaway is simple.

Start making yourself replaceable long before you sell. Document processes. Build a team that runs things. The more the business works without you, the easier it funds.

Buyers notice. Lenders notice. Your final price notices too.
