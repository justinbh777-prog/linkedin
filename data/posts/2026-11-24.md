# POST 2 — Personal Post

**Topic:** Why building a business that runs without you is the best exit strategy

---

The business that needs you most is worth the least.

I've seen this play out over 100+ times. An owner builds something great, but they are the business. They run the schedule. They handle the big clients. They fix every problem.

Then they come to me ready to sell.

And the first thing a buyer asks is: what happens when you leave?

That question sets the price. Not revenue. Not years in business. How much the operation depends on one person.

Last year I worked with an owner doing strong numbers. Good location. Loyal customers. But he worked 60 hours a week and nothing moved without him.

A buyer would basically be buying a job, not a business. That cut his valuation hard.

Compare that to an owner with half the revenue but a manager who ran daily operations. She sold for more and closed faster.

The takeaway is simple.

Start building the business to run without you years before you sell. Hire the manager. Write down the systems. Step back on purpose.

Your best exit starts long before the exit.
