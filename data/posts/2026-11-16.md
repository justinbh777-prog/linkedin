# POST 2 — Personal Post

**Topic:** The valuation mistake I see franchise owners make over and over

---

Most franchise owners think their business is worth more than it is.

Not because they're greedy. Because they're measuring the wrong thing.

Here's the mistake I see over and over.

Owners value their business based on revenue. "We did $2 million last year, so we should get a great price."

But buyers don't buy revenue. They buy profit. More specifically, they buy what's left after you pay yourself a fair wage for the work you do.

I had a seller last year who ran three units. Big top-line numbers. But once we added back his real role, the actual cash flow was thin. He was shocked.

The gap between what he thought and what the market said was huge.

The flip side happens too. I've seen owners with modest revenue who run clean, lean operations sell for a premium because their margins were strong.

So here's the takeaway.

Your value isn't your sales. It's your profit, and how much of it a buyer can keep without you in the building.
