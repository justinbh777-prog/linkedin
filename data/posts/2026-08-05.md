The biggest buyers in fitness aren't chasing the big cities. They're buying up the small ones first.

Fitness Ventures just bought 22 Crunch gyms and became the largest Crunch franchisee in the country.

But here's the part most people missed.

They spent 10 years building their base in mid-sized towns and college markets. The small stuff. Then they used that platform to move into LA and Houston.

That's the pattern. Build in the overlooked markets, then trade up.

For a 1-10 unit owner, this matters more than you think.

Buyers like this need well-run locations in smaller markets to fuel their growth. That means your gym, your studio, your service business could be exactly what they're hunting for.

As someone who works with franchise buyers and sellers every day, I can tell you the best exits rarely come from the flashiest markets.

Big buyers pay for the platform they can build on top of you. Your job is to be that clean piece.

**Topic:** Why your franchise is worth more than you think - or less

---

Most franchise owners guess what their business is worth. They're almost always wrong.

Some think their business is a goldmine. Others think it's worth nothing.

The truth usually sits somewhere in the middle, and it comes down to things owners don't expect.

It's not just about revenue. Buyers look at how much of the business depends on you.

If you're the one holding everything together, that scares buyers. It lowers your value.

But if you have a strong manager and clean books, buyers pay a premium.

I worked with an owner who ran three units and thought he'd get a low offer. His numbers looked average.

But he had built a team that ran the day-to-day without him. He barely worked in the business.

That one thing pushed his sale price way above what he expected.

The lesson is simple. Your value isn't only what you earn. It's how well the business runs without you.
